# Main Figure Captions — JCTC v0.3

The main figures are numbered in order of first appearance in the seven-section manuscript narrative.

## Figure 1. Pointwise reconstruction error and downstream chemical fidelity are distinct contracts

Schematic of the evaluation framework. An original electron-density field \(\rho\) is compressed and reconstructed as \(\tilde\rho\) under a codec-specified pointwise error bound. Field-level fidelity is characterized by the realized reconstruction error, including \(L_\infty=\|\tilde\rho-\rho\|_\infty\). Chemical fidelity is evaluated after rerunning Bader analysis on the reconstructed field. In the fixed-basin diagnostic, \(\tilde\rho\) is integrated over the reference partition \(\mathcal B(\rho)\); in the re-derived path, the partition \(\mathcal B(\tilde\rho)\) is reconstructed from the decompressed field before atomic charges are compared. The per-atom charge perturbation separates into integrand and domain-migration contributions. Certification additionally requires the reference Bader observable to be numerically resolvable at the stated charge contract under Protocol A.1.

## Figure 2. Nominal tolerance separates into bound utilization and a residual associated with reconstruction structure

**a,** Distribution of measured \(L_\infty\) divided by the requested absolute pointwise bound. Median utilization is 0.1575 for ZFP and approximately 1.000 for SZ3 and SPERR. **b,** Conservative complete-case comparison at the central Protocol-A.1 contract \(\tau=10^{-3}\) e. Operating points are matched within material by mutual-nearest measured \(L_\infty\) within 0.10 decades after removal of every material with any registered downstream failure at nominal relative tolerance \(\le0.01\). Median re-derived Bader-error ratios are 1.82 (95% CI 1.68–2.01; 119 materials) for SZ3/ZFP and 2.00 (1.79–2.25; 115 materials) for SPERR/ZFP. Within-material common-support interpolation gives 2.07 and 1.99, respectively. **c,** Material-fixed-effect codec multipliers on the same complete-case scale before and after inclusion of Bader voxel reassignment. The approximately twofold residual at matched realized error approaches unity when partition reassignment is included, connecting the matched-error comparison to the basin-migration mechanism.

## Figure 3. Reusing the reference Bader partition systematically understates downstream charge error

**a,** Re-derived versus fixed-basin maximum per-atom Bader-charge error for 4,627 successful base-ladder reconstructions. The dashed line denotes equality; re-derived error exceeds fixed-basin error in 99.7% of rows. **b,** Median paired re-derived/fixed-basin error ratio at the three core nominal relative tolerances, stratified by codec. Error bars denote 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. At nominal relative tolerance \(10^{-3}\), median ratios are 35.3× for SPERR, 2.9× for SZ3, and 70.7× for ZFP. **c,** Fraction of successful rows for which re-derived error exceeds fixed-basin error at the same core tolerances. The fixed-basin path isolates the within-reference-domain contribution, whereas the re-derived path measures the chemical analysis applied to the reconstructed field.

## Figure 4. Bader fidelity requires a numerically resolvable reference analysis and an algorithm-aware stability probe

**a,** Fraction of systems classified as non-evaluable under Protocol A.1 at Bader-charge contracts \(\tau\in\{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2}\}\) e, shown overall and by corpus stratum. Across all 319 systems, 79.9%, 41.4%, and 9.7% are non-evaluable at the three contracts. **b,** Bader stability floor under the archived float32 round-trip probe and the calibrated five-seed matched-amplitude uniform-noise probe for the 18-material calibration set. **c,** Algorithmically relevant effects of the two perturbation families. Float32 rounding creates a median 82 exact neighboring ties, whereas matched-amplitude random noise creates none; zero voxel reassignment occurs in 9/18 materials under float32 rounding and 2/18 under noise. **d,** Predictability audit for Protocol-A.1 eligibility using conventional low-cost metadata. Error bars show development fivefold-cross-validation AUROC variability; external AUROC is 0.409, 0.387, and 0.398 at \(10^{-4}\), \(10^{-3}\), and \(10^{-2}\) e, respectively. Excluding the known symmetry-equivalent basin-relabeling case changes the overall non-evaluable fraction by less than 0.3 percentage points at each contract.

## Figure 5. Basin migration links reconstruction structure to Bader-charge error

**a,** Direct decomposition of the maximum-error atom in a representative 12-material mechanism set into within-reference-domain integrand error and domain-migration error. Across 106 successful material–codec–tolerance cases, \(\Delta Q_{\rm total}=\Delta Q_{\rm integrand}+\Delta Q_{\rm domain}\) closes to within \(2.22\times10^{-16}\) e. The bounded domain fraction \(|\Delta Q_{\rm domain}|/(|\Delta Q_{\rm domain}|+|\Delta Q_{\rm integrand}|)\) has median 0.995 (IQR 0.965–0.999), with 84.9% of cases above 0.90. **b,** Fraction of grid voxels whose Bader label changes after reconstruction as a function of measured \(L_\infty\), showing that similar field-error magnitudes can produce different amounts of partition migration. **c,** Representative spatial comparison of reference and reconstructed Bader labels, highlighting voxels that change atomic basin. **d,** Material-fixed-effect mechanism-consistency analysis at the Protocol-A.1 \(10^{-3}\) e contract on the conservative complete-case material set. Codec multipliers controlling measured \(L_\infty\) are 2.34 for SZ3/ZFP and 2.08 for SPERR/ZFP; after inclusion of the reassigned-voxel fraction, the corresponding multipliers are 0.96 and 0.94. The reassignment coefficient is 0.880 (95% CI 0.723–1.037) in log10–log10 units, \(p=5.78\times10^{-28}\). Panels a and d provide complementary direct and population-scale evidence for domain migration as the dominant structural error channel.

## Figure 6. Certified compression forms a contract-dependent compression–coverage frontier

Median best certified compression ratio versus certification coverage among Protocol-A.1-admitted material–codec rows, shown separately for bulk and slab systems. Each point corresponds to a Bader-charge contract \(\tau\). At \(10^{-2}\) e, SZ3 reaches median best certified ratios of 51.8× for bulk and 67.8× for slabs, while ZFP provides higher coverage (98.8% bulk and 96.7% slab versus 95.2% and 77.0% for SZ3). At \(10^{-3}\) e, SZ3 and ZFP have similar bulk ratios (12.9× and 13.5×), while ZFP retains higher slab coverage. At \(10^{-4}\) e, ZFP reaches a 7.6× bulk ratio and certifies all 42 admitted bulk rows. Four slab materials are admitted at \(10^{-4}\) e, providing a small-sample view of the strictest slab frontier. Non-evaluable systems are removed at the reference-analysis eligibility stage before the compression–coverage frontier is constructed.
