# Supporting Information

## Chemical Fidelity beyond Pointwise Error Bounds: Stability and Bader-Domain Migration in Lossy-Compressed Electron Densities

**JCTC Supporting Information v0.2 — 2026-09-06**

---

## S1. Benchmark scope and reporting semantics

The development compression benchmark contains 254 DFT electron-density fields drawn from the Materials Project [1] and 6,343 successful compressed reconstructions. The independent stability corpus additionally includes AFLOW [2] and NOMAD [3] systems, giving 319 systems for the numerical-resolvability analysis. Of the successful reconstructions, 4,627 belong to the base tolerance ladder and 1,716 to the tighter ladder used to resolve strict operating points. Bader partitions are evaluated with BaderKit [4], while the three compression families are ZFP [5], SZ3 [6], and SPERR [7]. Successful reconstructions are stored in `benchmark/master_benchmark_full.csv`; registered downstream exceptions are retained separately in `failure_registry.csv`. The registry contains 77 entries, of which 76 are Bader-solver failures and one is a symmetry-equivalent basin-relabeling event.

A material-codec operating point is **certified** at a Bader-charge contract \(\tau\) only if (i) the material is Protocol-A.1 eligible at \(\tau\), (ii) the reconstruction respects its pointwise bound, and (iii) the re-derived maximum atom-wise Bader-charge error is below \(\tau\). `NON_EVALUABLE_BADER_UNSTABLE` denotes a material whose reference Bader analysis is not numerically resolvable to the requested contract under Protocol A.1; such a case is neither a codec pass nor a codec failure.

All 6,343 successful reconstructions respect their requested pointwise bound.

## S2. Reusing reference Bader basins systematically understates downstream error

For each successful base-ladder reconstruction we compared two downstream error definitions. The fixed-basin diagnostic integrates the reconstructed density over the reference Bader partition, whereas the primary re-derived error recomputes the Bader partition from the reconstructed density before comparing atomic charges. Across all 4,627 successful base-ladder rows, the re-derived error exceeds the fixed-basin error in **99.70%** of cases.

The pooled median paired ratio is 52.8×, but this multiplier is not used as a universal headline because it varies strongly with codec and tolerance. We therefore tested whether the large ratio is generated by very small fixed-basin denominators. No fixed-basin errors are below \(10^{-8}\) e, 4.4% are at or below \(10^{-6}\) e, and 19.5% are at or below \(10^{-5}\) e. Imposing denominator floors leaves the pooled ratio essentially unchanged through \(10^{-6}\) e:

| denominator floor for fixed-basin error | median re-derived/fixed ratio | 95% bootstrap CI |
|---:|---:|---:|
| \(10^{-15}\) e | 52.8× | 49.3–57.8 |
| \(10^{-8}\) e | 52.8× | 49.3–57.8 |
| \(10^{-6}\) e | 52.6× | 49.2–57.6 |
| \(10^{-5}\) e | 45.0× | 42.2–47.7 |
| \(10^{-4}\) e | 21.8× | 20.6–23.1 |

A material-balanced analysis gives a median of within-material median ratios of **59.5× [50.8, 70.3]** across 254 materials. The factor is highly codec dependent: 118.0× [87.2, 167.7] for SPERR, 7.8× [6.5, 9.4] for SZ3, and 117.2× [99.4, 131.7] for ZFP.

The codec/tolerance dependence is also visible on the core nominal ladder. At relative tolerance \(10^{-3}\), the median paired ratio is 35.3× for SPERR, 2.9× for SZ3, and 70.7× for ZFP. At \(10^{-2}\), the corresponding ratios are 10.3×, 1.7×, and 30.4×. These results support the denominator-robust directional conclusion—reusing the reference partition almost always understates Bader error—while showing why a single multiplicative factor should not be interpreted as invariant across operating conditions.

## S3. Protocol A.1 replaced an order-preserving stability probe

The original frozen stability protocol (Protocol A) used a float64→float32→float64 round trip as a nominally negligible perturbation. The probe was later found to be structurally inappropriate for an on-grid watershed: rounding is monotone for unequal values, preserves local rank almost everywhere, and creates exact neighboring ties that are resolved deterministically. In the 18-material calibration set, the float32 probe creates a median of 82 exact neighboring ties. Zero voxel reassignment occurs in 9/18 calibration materials under float32 rounding but only 2/18 under matched-amplitude random noise.

The failure is large enough to alter the scientific eligibility classification. On `mp-1007755` (HfAu), the archived float32 probe produces a Bader floor of \(5.9\times10^{-10}\) e with zero reassigned voxels. Uniform noise at the same \(L_\infty\) amplitude reassigns 364 voxels and moves a Bader charge by \(1.6\times10^{-3}\) e. Even noise at one hundredth of that amplitude reassigns 361 voxels and produces a \(1.2\times10^{-3}\) e Bader change.

Across all 319 stability systems, replacing the float32 probe by single-seed matched-amplitude random noise raises the median floor by a factor of approximately **8,700**. The non-evaluable fractions are:

| Bader contract \(\tau\) | Protocol A, archived float32 probe | random noise, single seed | Protocol A.1, max over five seeds |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(10^{-4}\) e | 23.2% | 69.0% | **79.9%** |
| \(10^{-3}\) e | 7.2% | 36.1% | **41.4%** |
| \(10^{-2}\) e | 2.2% | 9.1% | **9.7%** |

Protocol A remains archived unchanged. Protocol A.1 changes only the stability probe and seed aggregation while retaining the threshold set, exclusion semantics, and reporting rules.

## S4. Seed and amplitude sensitivity of Protocol A.1

Five random seeds were tested in the 18-material calibration study. The per-material floor spans a median of **0.47 decades** and up to **2.4 decades** across seeds; the eligibility verdict changes with the seed in 3/18 materials at \(\tau=10^{-4}\) e. Protocol A.1 therefore preregisters five seeds and defines the stability floor conservatively as the maximum response over seeds.

On the full 319-system corpus, using the five-seed maximum rather than a single seed changes the eligibility verdict for **35**, **17**, and **2** systems at \(10^{-4}\), \(10^{-3}\), and \(10^{-2}\) e, respectively. The full-corpus seed spread of the floor has a median of 0.37 decades, a 90th percentile of 0.88 decades, and a maximum of 3.8 decades.

The floor also depends on perturbation amplitude. Across a 0.1× to 10× amplitude range, the floor shifts by a median of **0.76 decades**; approximately half the calibration materials display a plateau while the remainder scale sublinearly. For this reason the manuscript uses the term **Protocol-A.1 numerical stability floor at the stated perturbation amplitude** rather than “intrinsic material noise floor.”

## S5. Protocol-A.1 resolvability and symmetry sensitivity

Across the 319-system stability corpus, the Protocol-A.1 non-evaluable fractions are **79.94%** at \(10^{-4}\) e, **41.38%** at \(10^{-3}\) e, and **9.72%** at \(10^{-2}\) e. These rates quantify whether Bader charge can serve as a meaningful contract at the requested precision; they are not codec failure rates.

One external system, `aflow-Al8Cu4U1_ICSD_601801`, has an extreme apparent floor caused by permutation of symmetry-equivalent Al basins rather than a change in the multiset of Bader charges. Removing this known relabeling pathology changes the corresponding non-evaluable fractions only to **79.87%**, **41.20%**, and **9.43%**. Thus the headline resolvability result is not driven by this symmetry-labeling event, although the event is retained separately in the failure taxonomy.

## S6. Codec bound utilization differs at equal nominal tolerance

All successful reconstructions respect their pointwise error bound, but the codecs use the requested error budget differently. Across all successful master-table rows, the median ratio of realized to nominal \(L_\infty\) error is **0.999987** for SPERR, **0.999999** for SZ3, and **0.157521** for ZFP. Consequently, a same-nominal comparison gives ZFP a substantially smaller realized field perturbation and cannot be interpreted as pure evidence of error-field structure.

The main manuscript therefore separates two effects: nominal-bound utilization and the residual downstream difference after matching by measured \(L_\infty\).

## S7. Conservative complete-case sensitivity of realized-\(L_\infty\) matching

Registered Bader failures are not uniformly distributed among codecs. At nominal relative tolerances \(\le 0.01\), the failure registry contains **24** failed rows across **20** unique materials: 20 ZFP failures and four SPERR failures. To prevent selective survival of successful rows from favoring a codec, we repeated the matched-realized-\(L_\infty\) analysis after removing the entire material whenever any codec had a registered low-tolerance failure. This reduces the development set from 254 to **234** materials.

The codec-associated residual remains approximately twofold across all three Bader contracts:

| Bader contract | comparison | 0.10-decade matching | common-support interpolation |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| \(10^{-4}\) e | SZ3/ZFP | 1.91× [1.49, 2.63], n=40 | 2.37× [2.06, 2.83], n=44 |
| \(10^{-4}\) e | SPERR/ZFP | 1.86× [1.61, 2.19], n=39 | 1.96× [1.66, 2.27], n=44 |
| \(10^{-3}\) e | SZ3/ZFP | **1.82× [1.68, 2.01]**, n=119 | 2.07× [1.95, 2.19], n=132 |
| \(10^{-3}\) e | SPERR/ZFP | **2.00× [1.79, 2.25]**, n=115 | 1.99× [1.83, 2.21], n=132 |
| \(10^{-2}\) e | SZ3/ZFP | 1.78× [1.68, 1.93], n=183 | 1.96× [1.80, 2.09], n=208 |
| \(10^{-2}\) e | SPERR/ZFP | 1.86× [1.72, 2.00], n=178 | 1.88× [1.70, 2.03], n=209 |

At the primary \(10^{-3}\) e contract, the median paired realized-\(L_\infty\) ratio is 0.965 for both SZ3/ZFP and SPERR/ZFP matches, showing that the retained QoI difference is not caused by a gross mismatch in pointwise perturbation magnitude.

## S8. Full-benchmark reassignment attenuation is consistent with the direct mechanism

At the \(10^{-3}\) e Protocol-A.1 contract, complete-case material-fixed-effect models were fitted on common realized-\(L_\infty\) support. The base model includes material identity, centered measured \(L_\infty\), and codec indicators. The reassignment model additionally includes the fraction of Bader voxels whose labels change after compression.

| model quantity | SZ3/ZFP | SPERR/ZFP |
|---|---:|---:|
| base model: material FE + realized \(L_\infty\) | **2.34×** [2.15, 2.53] | **2.08×** [1.91, 2.26] |
| after adding voxel reassignment | **0.96×** [0.83, 1.10] | **0.94×** [0.82, 1.07] |

The log–log reassignment coefficient is **0.880 [0.723, 1.037]**, \(p=5.78\times10^{-28}\). The codec-associated residual is therefore strongly attenuated after adding a variable that directly records movement of the field-derived Bader partition. This model is used as a **mechanism-consistency diagnostic** on the material-level analysis set, with reassignment treated as a post-compression structural descriptor.

## S9. Global electron-count deviation does not explain the codec-associated residual

We tested global electron-count deviation as an alternative mechanism on the same complete-case, Protocol-A.1-eligible \(10^{-3}\) e analysis set. The model contains 3,925 rows from 132 materials on common measured-\(L_\infty\) support.

Adding absolute electron-count deviation does not attenuate the codec terms. For SZ3/ZFP, the multiplier changes from **2.34× [2.15, 2.53]** to **2.58× [2.23, 2.97]**; for SPERR/ZFP it changes from **2.08× [1.91, 2.26]** to **2.06× [1.90, 2.24]**. By contrast, adding reassignment changes the multipliers to 0.96× and 0.94×.

When electron-count deviation and reassignment are included together, the electron-count coefficient is \(-0.017\) [−0.040, 0.006] with \(p=0.139\), whereas the reassignment coefficient remains **0.879 [0.722, 1.036]**, \(p=5.25\times10^{-28}\). These results argue against a simple global integral/conservation error as the explanation for the approximately twofold codec-associated residual.

## S10. Direct domain/integrand decomposition in the representative mechanism set

For an atom \(a\), the charge perturbation is decomposed as

\[
\Delta Q_a=\Delta Q_{a,\mathrm{integrand}}+\Delta Q_{a,\mathrm{domain}}.
\]

Because cancellation can make \(|\Delta Q_{\mathrm{domain}}|/|\Delta Q_{\mathrm{total}}|>1\), mechanism dominance is summarized with the bounded fraction

\[
f_{\mathrm{domain}}=\frac{|\Delta Q_{\mathrm{domain}}|}{|\Delta Q_{\mathrm{domain}}|+|\Delta Q_{\mathrm{integrand}}|}.
\]

The current representative mechanism set contains 12 materials and 106 successful material–codec–tolerance cases. At the atom of maximum total charge error, the algebraic decomposition closes to within \(2.22\times10^{-16}\) e. The median bounded domain contribution is **0.995**, the interquartile range is **0.965–0.999**, and **84.9%** of cases exceed a domain share of 0.90.

The direct decomposition defines the mechanism at the representative-set level. The full-benchmark reassignment analysis in S8 provides an independent material-scale test of the same domain-migration mechanism across the broader compression benchmark.

## S11. Certified compression frontier is contract dependent

For each Protocol-A.1-admitted material and codec, the best compression ratio among operating points with re-derived Bader error below the stated contract was recorded. Compression ratio and certification coverage are reported jointly because a high ratio is not useful if a large fraction of admitted systems lack a certified operating point.

### Bulk systems

| contract | SPERR ratio / coverage | SZ3 ratio / coverage | ZFP ratio / coverage |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(10^{-2}\) e, n=168 | 11.5× / 95.8% | **51.8× / 95.2%** | 30.0× / **98.8%** |
| \(10^{-3}\) e, n=103 | 6.17× / 99.0% | 12.9× / 99.0% | **13.5× / 99.0%** |
| \(10^{-4}\) e, n=42 | 4.23× / 90.5% | 6.33× / 85.7% | **7.63× / 100%** |

| contract | SPERR ratio / coverage | SZ3 ratio / coverage | ZFP ratio / coverage |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|

## S12. Reproducibility and submitted scientific snapshot

The reproducibility package is organized around an immutable scientific snapshot containing the benchmark tables, failure registry, Protocol A.1 stability tables, probe-calibration data, material provenance metadata, mechanism data used in the paper, supplementary sensitivity tables, statistical scripts, and figure-generation inputs. The submitted archive is tied to a frozen Git commit and persistent identifier.

The scientific snapshot records codec versions, the Bader implementation and version, exact Protocol A.1 seeds and perturbation definition, source-data checksums and available licenses, the submitted Git commit SHA, release tag, archive identifier, and hashes for the manuscript, Supporting Information, TOC graphic, and final figure files.
