## Q1 - is the perturbation sensitivity BaderKit-specific?

No. `stability_comparison.csv`, `mechanism_noise_floors.csv`.

- Henkelman ongrid reproduces the BaderKit ongrid five-seed noise floor to
  print precision (1e-6 e) on 10 of 12 representatives, and the codec
  responses with a median ratio of 1.00 (IQR 0.92-1.005).
- The two exceptions, `mp-1079749` and `mp-30772`, are the two materials whose
  *unperturbed* per-atom charges already disagree between implementations by
  1.5 e and 2.4 e (all other ongrid baseline differences are 0 except
  `nomad-0kvBRPSc8RU6`, 0.20 e). BaderKit persistence merging changes the
  basin set there. Their A.1 floors are therefore implementation-dependent in
  magnitude, but both remain non-evaluable at 1e-4 and 1e-3 under Henkelman
  ongrid as well.
- Henkelman neargrid floors differ in value but keep the stratification:
  high-floor materials stay high (0.011-0.032 e), low-floor stay at or below
  ~1e-4 e. Neargrid codec responses relative to BaderKit have median 0.77
  (IQR 0.31-1.22).
- Codec ordering is implementation-independent: at relative 1e-4 the
  SZ3/ZFP Bader-error ratio is 6.2x (BaderKit), 4.8x (Henkelman ongrid),
  3.2x (Henkelman neargrid); SPERR/ZFP 6.7x / 5.5x / 2.9x.

## Q2 - does rearranging the same error-value multiset change the response?

Yes, moderately, codec- and stratum-dependently. `mechanism_spatial_pairs.csv`
(36 material x codec pairs per solver and control; sentinel excluded).

| control | BaderKit median log2(control/codec) | fraction increased | Henkelman ongrid | neargrid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| global permutation | 0.26 | 53 % | 0.24 | 0.07 |
| periodic shift | 0.02 | 50 % | 0.04 | 0.00 |
| density-stratified permutation | 0.26 | 64 % | 0.15 | 0.07 |

- By codec (all solvers): SZ3 errors grow ~1.3-1.4x when rearranged
  (61-75 % increase), ZFP 1.1-1.3x, SPERR does not grow (31-42 %).
- By stratum (BaderKit): low-floor materials ~1.5x (0.0027 -> 0.0056 e);
  high-floor materials unchanged (0.148 -> 0.147-0.25 e), where basin
  instability already dominates.
- The periodic shift is a null: preserving the error field's autocorrelation
  and only changing its alignment with the density produces no effect. The
  effect requires destroying spatial autocorrelation. This supports
  sensitivity to error organization under permutation, not a boundary-local
  causal claim.
- Integrity: control L-inf matches the source codec error to 3.7e-12
  relative; the global permutation moves error into vacuum and adds up to
  15.6 % negative-density voxels, so it is a stress control; the stratified
  permutation is the cleaner control and agrees in direction.
