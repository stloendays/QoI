# Reviewer risk register — QoI manuscript

Updated 2026-09-06 after realized-L∞, complete-case and mechanism-attenuation analyses. Branch-only editorial document.

| Risk / likely reviewer objection | Severity | Current evidence / response | Remaining action |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **“ZFP looks better only because its realized error is much smaller than the requested bound.”** | High | True in part: ZFP median bound utilization is 0.1575 vs ~1.0 for SZ3/SPERR. Addressed by matched measured-L∞ analysis, A.1 qualification, material fixed effects, multiple calipers and common-support interpolation. Residual remains ~2x. | Main Figure 5 must explicitly separate bound utilization from residual codec association. Never attribute the full same-nominal 6–12x gap to structure. |
| **“The realized-L∞ matching result depends on an arbitrary caliper/matching method.”** | High | At tau=1e-3 e, SZ3/ZFP remains 1.74–1.85x across 0.05–0.15 dex calipers; SPERR/ZFP 1.99–2.11x. Common-support interpolation gives 2.08x and 2.04x. | Put extended calipers/interpolation in S10; one primary 0.10-dex result in main text. |
| **“Codec-specific Bader-solver failures selectively censor difficult ZFP cases.”** | High | 24 failures at nominal relative <=0.01 across 20 materials (ZFP 20, SPERR 4). Conservative complete-case removal of all 20 affected materials leaves tau=1e-3 matched ratios 1.82x and 2.00x; interpolation 2.07x and 1.99x. | Report the non-random failure distribution explicitly; never call the successful-row set failure-free. |
| **“Bader charge itself is too numerically unstable to serve as a fidelity target.”** | High | This is a central result, not a hidden weakness. Protocol A.1 defines eligibility; 41.4% non-evaluable at 1e-3 e and 79.9% at 1e-4 e. Codec certification uses `eligible AND error<tau`. | Maintain NON_EVALUABLE semantics separate from pass/fail in every figure/table. |
| **“The stability probe is arbitrary.”** | High | Archived Protocol A exposed a blind spot: float32 rounding is monotone/order-preserving. Replacement probe was calibrated on 18 materials before A.1 was frozen; 5 preregistered seeds, amplitude stated, S4 amplitude sensitivity. | In Methods/Discussion call the A.1 floor **probe-defined at a stated amplitude**, never an intrinsic material constant. |
| **“The ~2x codec residual may be a statistical artifact rather than a physical mechanism.”** | High | Direct 12-material decomposition: bounded domain dominance median 0.995. Full-table attenuation: codec multipliers ~2x -> ~1 after adding reassigned-voxel fraction; complete-case version 2.34->0.96 and 2.08->0.94. | Phrase attenuation as mechanism-consistent, not causal mediation. Figure 3 should juxtapose direct decomposition with full-table attenuation. |
| **“Only 12 materials have direct mechanism decomposition; you cannot generalize to all 254.”** | High | Agreed. Claim is restricted to the representative mechanism set. Full-table reassignment attenuation supplies complementary evidence but is not the same as direct decomposition. | Once per-atom file lands, use case/material aggregation and leave-one-material-out; do not exploit atom count as fake N. |
| **“The fixed-basin understatement factor is inflated by division by near-zero fixed-basin errors.”** | High | Direction is robust: resolved > fixed in 99.7% of successful base rows. Raw pooled ratio median 52.8x may be denominator-sensitive. | Denominator-robust ratio audit is scripted and running. Use directional headline unless a multiplicative summary survives floor/material-balanced sensitivity. |
| **“The novelty is already covered by QoI-preserving compression.”** | High | Jiao et al. already establish raw-error/QoI gap. Manuscript novelty is narrowed to field-derived integration domains, stability qualification/probe validation, realized-bound utilization separation, and Bader-specific basin migration. | Cite PVLDB QoI work prominently; remove any “first pointwise-vs-QoI” language. |
| **“TopoSZ/topology-aware compression already solves this.”** | High | TopoSZ/general topological frameworks preserve specified topological descriptors. 2026 local-order-preserving compression is especially close because Bader follows local ascent ordering. Present paper evaluates general-purpose pointwise-bounded codecs and identifies chemistry-specific failure mechanism; it does not claim no order-preserving solution exists. | Discuss 2026 Local-Order-Preserving Compressor, TopoSZp, Morse–Smale preservation. Consider a topology-aware supplementary baseline only if practical before submission. |
| **“Spatial error correlation is already known to affect QoIs.”** | Medium | TOPIQ (SC 2026) explicitly models spatial error correlation/data-error coupling. Our contribution is the discontinuous field-derived-domain mechanism and direct re-partitioning evaluation, not generic correlation awareness. | Cite TOPIQ as contemporary related work and sharpen distinction. |
| **“Why not use a different charge-partitioning method?”** | Medium | Current work makes a method-specific claim about on-grid Bader partitioning, not universal atomic charge analysis. | State scope explicitly; optional future work can compare Hirshfeld/Voronoi-like fixed/geometric partitions to test whether the domain-migration failure is specific to density-derived partitions. |
| **“Why no full DFT reruns / energies / forces?”** | Medium | Scientific question is preservation of stored charge-density-derived Bader QoI; source fields are public and no new DFT is needed for this evaluation. | Keep scope narrow; do not imply conclusions for self-consistent energies/forces after compression. |
| **“External validation only covers stability, not the 3-codec chemical benchmark.”** | Medium | Correct. External AFLOW/NOMAD strata test the generality of Bader resolvability/probe findings, not the complete compression frontier. | Distinguish external validation targets clearly. Do not say the complete codec ranking was externally validated. |
| **“Early stopping produces codec-specific ladder truncation.”** | Medium | Tight ladder closes strict-contract censoring for A.1-eligible materials; matched analyses also restrict to <=0.01 in reviewer stress tests. | Explain early-stop rule and tight-rung completion in Methods; use common-support/interpolation sensitivity in supplement. |
| **“The symmetry-equivalent 2.15 e floor is a label permutation, not chemistry.”** | Medium | Correctly classified as `basin_relabelling_symmetry_equivalent`; non-evaluable for a position-indexed QoI and not treated as a 2 e chemical perturbation. | Keep taxonomy explicit; per-atom plan includes equivalence-class sensitivity where relevant. |
| **“The paper proposes no new compressor.”** | Medium | The contribution is an evaluation/certification framework and a measured failure mechanism. Negative algorithm track is retained as supplementary evidence rather than forced into a codec contribution. | Target venues/review framing that accept methodological evaluation, not only new compression algorithms. |
| **“BQB/other electronic-density compressors are omitted.”** | Low–Medium | BQB is lossless/precision-selectable volumetric compression with a different denominator/task and is retained as related work; unit conversion is documented. | Do not claim exhaustive codec benchmarking. Keep fair-comparison rationale in supplement. |

## Submission gate

Before calling the paper submission-ready, require:
1. per-atom mechanism file committed and integrity-checked against the 106-case summary;
2. denominator-robust fixed-basin understatement wording frozen;
3. final Figure 3 and Figure 5 generated from committed scripts/tables;
4. 2026 related-work paragraph integrated into the manuscript;
5. all main-text numbers mapped to one committed evidence file and one explicit denominator/eligibility definition.
