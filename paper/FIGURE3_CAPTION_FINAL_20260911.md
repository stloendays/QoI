# Figure 3 caption — submission storyline revision (2026-09-12)

## Primary caption

**Figure 3 | QoI Stability Qualification prospectively stratifies downstream numerical risk after equalizing compression-search opportunity.** **A,** The frozen five-seed QoI Stability Qualification (QSQ) gate is challenged prospectively at the pre-specified primary threshold of $10^{-3}\,e$ using 59 fresh iid-uniform perturbations per development material, with no retuning of the gate, amplitudes or threshold. QSQ admits 143/254 materials (56.3% coverage). Among 8,437 fresh trials from admitted materials, 135 exceed the threshold (1.600%; material-cluster 95% CI 0.782–2.596%), whereas 5,326/6,549 trials from the 111 screen-rejected materials exceed it (81.325%; 75.981–86.257%), giving a rejected-to-eligible risk ratio of 50.83. **B,** The association is not an artifact of unequal codec search opportunity. Completing the same four tight settings for the 111 previously uncovered materials adds 1,332/1,332 successful reconstructions. Under this completed common-tight ladder, the risk of finding no numerical pass at $10^{-3}\,e$ is 3.3% for QSQ-eligible versus 66.4% for screen-rejected material–codec pairs (20.34-fold); the same direction persists at $10^{-4}\,e$ (10.9% versus 79.3%) and $10^{-2}\,e$ (0% versus 68.0%). **C,** Prospective fresh-perturbation discrimination remains strong at the two pre-specified secondary thresholds: 4.016% versus 86.938% at $10^{-4}\,e$ (21.65-fold) and 0.148% versus 79.593% at $10^{-2}\,e$ (537.69-fold). The same 14,986 physical perturbation trials support all three thresholds. QSQ is therefore interpreted as a prospectively validated empirical risk-stratification screen under the declared perturbation model, not as a worst-case stability certificate or a guarantee of new-material generalization.

## Short caption option

**Figure 3 | A frozen QSQ gate prospectively separates low- and high-risk downstream responses.** At the primary $10^{-3}\,e$ endpoint, 59 unseen perturbations per material yield a 1.600% exceedance risk in the 143 QSQ-admitted materials versus 81.325% in the 111 screen-rejected materials (50.83-fold). After equalizing codec search opportunity with 1,332/1,332 additional tight-ladder reconstructions, no-pass risk remains 3.3% versus 66.4% (20.34-fold), showing that the separation is not explained by unequal search depth.

## Interpretation boundary

Do not use the historical 97.2% / 95.5% full-record reclassification fractions as the Figure 3 headline. Those values are retained only as ladder-design-sensitive provenance. Do not describe QSQ as rescuing codec failures, proving causality for an individual reconstruction error, certifying worst-case stability, or generalizing prospectively to unseen materials. The primary Figure 3 claim is prospective risk stratification after a separate equal-search control.
