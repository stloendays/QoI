## Replacement Abstract

Error-bounded lossy compression is increasingly used to reduce the storage and I/O cost of scientific fields, yet a bound on reconstructed field values need not bound the error of a downstream scientific observable. We examine this distinction for Bader charges derived from density-functional-theory charge densities using 6,343 successful compressed reconstructions of 254 materials, three general-purpose pointwise-error-bounded compressors (SZ3, ZFP and SPERR), and an independent 319-system stability corpus. A common evaluation shortcut—integrating reconstructed densities over Bader basins derived from the original field—suppresses changes in the integration domains. Across 4,627 successful base-ladder reconstructions, the Bader error obtained after re-deriving the partition exceeds the fixed-basin estimate in 99.7% of cases, with a median resolved-to-fixed error ratio of 52.8. Direct error decomposition in a 12-material representative mechanism set closes to machine precision and gives a median bounded domain-migration contribution of 0.995, indicating that changes in the field-derived integration domains dominate the measured maximum-atom error in that set.

Codec differences at equal nominal tolerance are partly explained by unequal use of the requested pointwise error budget: ZFP realizes a median of 0.158 of its nominal bound, whereas SZ3 and SPERR nearly saturate theirs. This magnitude effect does not explain the full downstream difference. Among systems whose Bader charges are numerically resolvable to 10^-3 e under a preregistered perturbation protocol, reconstructions matched within 0.10 decades of measured L∞ error retain median Bader-error ratios of 1.90 [1.70, 2.20] for SZ3/ZFP and 1.87 [1.63, 2.08] for SPERR/ZFP. The result is insensitive to matching strategy and to conservative removal of every material with any registered downstream failure at nominal relative tolerance <=0.01: in that complete-case analysis the corresponding ratios are 1.82 [1.68, 2.01] and 2.00 [1.79, 2.25]. Material-fixed-effect models show an approximately twofold codec-associated residual after controlling measured L∞; this residual attenuates to approximately unity after adding the fraction of reassigned Bader voxels, consistent with basin migration linking codec-specific reconstruction structure to charge error.

A second limitation precedes compression itself: the downstream observable is not numerically resolvable at every requested accuracy. Under Protocol A.1, which uses the maximum response to five preregistered non-monotone perturbations at a stated amplitude, 41.4% of 319 systems are non-evaluable at a 10^-3 e Bader-charge contract and 79.9% at 10^-4 e. Conventional system descriptors do not transfer as useful predictors of this eligibility to untouched external data. Despite these constraints, lossy compression remains useful, but the preferred codec depends on the chemical contract: SZ3 leads at 10^-2 e, ZFP and SZ3 are comparable near 10^-3 e, and ZFP leads at 10^-4 e. These results motivate a stability-qualified evaluation framework in which the downstream analysis is re-executed, realized rather than nominal field error is measured, and the quantity of interest is shown to be numerically defined before fidelity is certified.

## Replacement Results subsection: Separating nominal tolerance, realized error magnitude and codec-associated structure

### Nominal pointwise tolerance conflates error-budget utilization with error structure

A comparison at equal requested tolerance is not, by itself, a comparison at equal perturbation magnitude. All 6,343 successful reconstructions satisfy their requested pointwise bound, but the three codecs occupy that bound differently. SZ3 and SPERR nearly saturate it, with median `realized_Linf / nominal_tolerance_absolute` values of 1.000 and 1.000, respectively. ZFP is substantially more conservative: its median utilization is 0.1575 (interquartile range 0.1323–0.1939). Consequently, the large Bader-error gaps observed at equal nominal tolerance contain a substantial magnitude component. For example, at nominal relative tolerances of 10^-4, 10^-3 and 10^-2, the median realized-L∞ ratio between SZ3 and ZFP is 5.85, 6.49 and 8.46, respectively. Same-nominal error ratios should therefore not be interpreted as pure evidence of spatial error structure.

To separate these effects, we compared codecs within each material after matching reconstructions by their measured L∞ error. With a mutual-nearest-neighbour caliper of 0.10 decades, the all-successful-row analysis yields a median SZ3/ZFP Bader-error ratio of 1.81 [1.70, 1.95] across 214 materials and a SPERR/ZFP ratio of 1.72 [1.54, 1.89] across 206 materials. The median matched L∞ ratios are 0.951 in both comparisons, so the residual is not explained by systematically larger matched perturbations.

Because Bader charge itself may be numerically unstable, we repeated the analysis only on materials admitted by Protocol A.1 at each charge-error contract. At tau = 10^-3 e, 0.10-decade matching retains 128 materials for SZ3/ZFP and 124 for SPERR/ZFP. The median resolved-charge-error ratios are 1.90 [1.70, 2.20] and 1.87 [1.63, 2.08], with the first codec producing the larger error in 91.4% and 79.8% of materials, respectively. Material-fixed-effect regressions controlling measured L∞ give consistent estimates: 2.23 [2.09, 2.39] for SZ3 relative to ZFP and 2.00 [1.86, 2.16] for SPERR relative to ZFP at the same eligibility contract.

The approximately twofold residual is robust to how common perturbation scale is defined. Restricting all codecs to nominal relative tolerance <=0.01 and tightening the matching caliper from 0.15 to 0.05 decades leaves the direction unchanged. At tau = 10^-3 e, the 0.10-decade estimates are 1.82 [1.68, 2.03] for SZ3/ZFP and 2.01 [1.84, 2.38] for SPERR/ZFP. Replacing nearest-neighbour matching by within-material log-log interpolation over the common realized-L∞ support gives 2.08 [1.96, 2.16] and 2.04 [1.88, 2.21]. Thus the result is not an artefact of a particular matching window.

These analyses establish an association, not a causal codec effect. They show that measured pointwise error magnitude is insufficient to explain the codec-dependent downstream error. The physical interpretation of that residual is addressed separately through the Bader-domain decomposition and voxel-reassignment analyses.

## New Results subsection: Basin reassignment accounts for the codec-associated residual

### Basin migration links reconstruction structure to downstream charge error

The direct decomposition identifies domain migration as the dominant contribution in the representative mechanism set, but it does not by itself show whether basin migration explains the codec-associated residual across the full benchmark. We therefore fitted material-fixed-effect models at the 10^-3 e Protocol-A.1 contract, first predicting log Bader error from log measured L∞ and codec identity, and then adding the fraction of voxels whose Bader-basin label changes after reconstruction. The analysis was restricted to nominal relative tolerance <=0.01 and to the common realized-L∞ support of all three codecs.

Before accounting for basin reassignment, the model gives codec multipliers of 2.32 for SZ3 relative to ZFP and 2.10 for SPERR relative to ZFP. Adding the reassigned-voxel fraction almost completely attenuates these coefficients, to 0.98 and 0.95, respectively. The reassignment term itself has a coefficient of 0.840 log10 units of Bader error per log10 unit of reassigned fraction (95% interval 0.691–0.988). In other words, after materials and measured pointwise magnitude are controlled, the quantity that most directly records changes in the field-derived partition statistically absorbs the approximately twofold codec-associated residual.

We treated this attenuation as mechanism-consistent rather than as a formal causal-mediation estimate. Basin reassignment is measured after compression and is not randomized; moreover, a simple voxel count does not encode which atoms exchange volume or how much density those voxels carry. The direct identity `Delta Q_total = Delta Q_integrand + Delta Q_domain` in the representative mechanism set remains the primary mechanistic evidence. The full-table attenuation supplies a complementary link between that decomposition and the codec-level pattern.

## New Results subsection: Registered failures do not explain the matched-L∞ result

### Conservative complete-case analysis closes the selective-survival concern

Downstream failures are not uniformly distributed across codecs. The failure registry contains 77 exceptional rows overall. At nominal relative tolerance <=0.01, 24 failures occur across 20 unique materials: 20 ZFP cases and four SPERR cases. Because failed rows are absent from the successful master table, an analysis based only on surviving reconstructions could, in principle, favour a codec by selectively removing difficult cases.

We therefore repeated the key comparisons after removing an entire material whenever any codec had any registered failure at nominal relative tolerance <=0.01. This conservative rule reduces the benchmark from 254 to 234 materials and removes successful rows from the other codecs as well, eliminating codec-specific survival within those materials. At the 10^-3 e Protocol-A.1 contract, the 0.10-decade matched Bader-error ratios remain 1.82 [1.68, 2.01] for SZ3/ZFP and 2.00 [1.79, 2.25] for SPERR/ZFP. Common-support interpolation gives 2.07 [1.95, 2.19] and 1.99 [1.83, 2.21]. The basin-reassignment attenuation also survives: SZ3/ZFP changes from 2.34 to 0.96 after adding reassignment, and SPERR/ZFP from 2.08 to 0.94; the reassignment coefficient is 0.880 [0.723, 1.037] with p = 5.78 x 10^-28. Registered low-tolerance failures therefore do not account for either the approximately twofold residual or its attenuation by basin migration.

## Replacement/expanded Related Work paragraph

Prior work already establishes that pointwise error control is not equivalent to downstream scientific fidelity. Jiao et al. developed QoI-preserving compression theory for several downstream quantities, explicitly identifying the gap between raw-data error guarantees and scientific-analysis error (PVLDB 16, 697–710; DOI 10.14778/3574245.3574255). A parallel line of work addresses topological fidelity. TopoSZ preserves extrema and contour-tree relations under error-bounded compression (IEEE TVCG 30, 1302–1312; DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2023.3326920), and subsequent frameworks augment generic compressors with topological guarantees (IEEE TVCG 31, 3693–3705; DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2025.3567054). Recent 2026 methods move still closer to the mechanism studied here: local-order-preserving compression explicitly preserves the relative ordering of neighbouring values (IEEE Transactions on Big Data; DOI 10.1109/TBDATA.2026.3705355), while TOPIQ models QoI uncertainty using spatial compression-error correlation and data–error coupling (arXiv:2608.26912; accepted SC 2026).

Our contribution is therefore not the generic observation that pointwise bounds can miss QoIs or topology. The setting studied here differs because the downstream quantity is evaluated over integration domains that are themselves reconstructed from the compressed field. For grid-based Bader analysis, basin assignment follows ascent relations on the charge-density field; perturbing the field can therefore alter the domain over which the charge is integrated. We explicitly separate this domain-migration term from within-domain density error, qualify whether the Bader charge is numerically resolvable before using it as a contract, validate the perturbation used for that qualification, and distinguish nominal error-budget utilization from residual codec-associated structure. Local-order-preserving and topology-aware compressors are consequently natural algorithmic directions suggested by the present evaluation, rather than evidence that the evaluation problem has already been resolved for Bader chemistry.

## Methods insertion: matched-realized-L∞ and complete-case sensitivity

For each material and codec pair, we compared reconstruction points using measured rather than requested L∞ error. The primary nonparametric analysis identifies mutual nearest neighbours in log10(realized L∞) and retains pairs separated by at most 0.10 decades. Ratios are first summarized within material and then across materials; uncertainty is obtained by material-level bootstrap resampling. Sensitivity analyses use calipers of 0.05, 0.075 and 0.15 decades and an alternative interpolation procedure that evaluates each codec on three points within the material-specific overlap of its log10(realized L∞) support. Protocol-A.1-qualified analyses restrict materials by the eligibility flag at the stated Bader-charge contract before matching.

To assess selective censoring from downstream failures, we additionally define a conservative complete-case set. Any material appearing in `failure_registry.csv` at nominal relative tolerance <=0.01 is removed in its entirety from all codecs, including its otherwise successful rows. This removes 20 unique materials and leaves 234 development materials. Complete-case matching and interpolation are then repeated without changing any other analysis choice.

For mechanism-consistency regressions, the outcome is log10 re-derived Bader error. Models contain material fixed effects, log10 measured L∞ and codec indicators; the expanded model adds log10 reassigned-voxel fraction, with a half-voxel pseudocount scaled by grid size to accommodate zero-reassignment cases. Standard errors are clustered by material. These models are interpreted as attenuation analyses rather than causal mediation because basin reassignment is a post-compression quantity.

## Discussion insertion: what the approximately twofold residual means

The comparison between nominal and realized error separates two effects that would otherwise be conflated. ZFP's strong performance at equal requested tolerance is partly mechanical: it uses much less of the allowed pointwise budget. Yet this does not exhaust the difference. Across several ways of constructing comparable realized perturbations, and after removing systems whose Bader charge is not resolvable at the requested contract, SZ3 and SPERR retain approximately twice the re-derived Bader error of ZFP. The near-complete disappearance of this codec-associated residual after conditioning on basin reassignment connects that statistical pattern to the direct error decomposition. A parsimonious interpretation is therefore that codec-specific reconstruction patterns differ in how strongly they perturb the ascent relations that define Bader domains, and that domain migration—not merely pointwise amplitude—converts those structural differences into charge error.

This interpretation also clarifies what the work does not show. We do not infer that one codec has an intrinsically universal chemical advantage, nor that a twofold multiplier transfers to arbitrary quantities of interest. The result is specific to the Bader partitioning pipeline and to the tested charge-density corpora. Likewise, the reassignment attenuation is not proof of a causal mediator. Its value is triangulation: a direct algebraic decomposition, voxel-level partition changes and material-controlled codec comparisons all point to the same failure mode.
