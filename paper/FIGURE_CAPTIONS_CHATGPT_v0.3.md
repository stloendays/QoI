# Main-figure captions — manuscript v0.3

These captions use the manuscript statistical hierarchy frozen in `STATISTICAL_ANALYSIS_PLAN_CHATGPT_v1.md`. Figure 3 remains provisional at the per-atom level until the full mechanism table is committed.

## Figure 1 | A pointwise reconstruction bound is not a downstream chemical-fidelity contract

Schematic of the evaluation framework. An original electron-density field \(\rho\) is compressed and reconstructed as \(\tilde\rho\) under a codec-specified pointwise error bound. Field-level fidelity is described by the measured reconstruction error, including \(L_\infty=\|\tilde\rho-\rho\|_\infty\), whereas chemical fidelity is evaluated after re-running the Bader analysis on the reconstructed field. Two diagnostic paths are distinguished. In the **fixed-basin** path, \(\tilde\rho\) is integrated over the original partition \(\mathcal B(\rho)\), which suppresses changes in the integration domains. In the **re-derived** path, the partition \(\mathcal B(\tilde\rho)\) is recomputed from the reconstruction before charges are compared. The total per-atom charge error can be decomposed as \(\Delta Q_{\rm total}=\Delta Q_{\rm integrand}+\Delta Q_{\rm domain}\). Scientific certification additionally requires the uncompressed QoI to be numerically resolvable at the requested charge contract under Protocol A.1. NON_EVALUABLE denotes a QoI-resolution failure and is distinct from a codec failure.

## Figure 2 | Reusing the original Bader partition systematically understates downstream error

**a,** Re-derived versus fixed-basin maximum per-atom Bader-charge error for the 4,627 successful base-ladder reconstructions. The dashed line denotes equality. Re-derived error exceeds fixed-basin error in 99.7% of successful rows. **b,** Median paired re-derived/fixed-basin error ratio at the three core nominal relative tolerances, stratified by codec; error bars denote material/row bootstrap 95% confidence intervals as defined by the corresponding analysis table. The magnitude of understatement is codec- and tolerance-dependent—for example, at nominal relative tolerance \(10^{-3}\), medians are approximately 35.3× for SPERR, 2.9× for SZ3 and 70.7× for ZFP—so no single multiplicative factor is treated as universal. **c,** Fraction of successful rows for which re-derived error exceeds fixed-basin error at the same core tolerances. Together, the panels show that fixed-domain evaluation removes an important error channel by construction and is retained only as a diagnostic. Data: `benchmark/master_benchmark_base_ladder.csv` and `paper_data_v03/fig2_fixed_vs_resolved_core_tolerances.csv`.

## Figure 3 | Basin migration is the mechanism linking reconstruction structure to Bader-charge error

**a,** Direct decomposition of the maximum-error atom in the representative 12-material mechanism set into within-reference-domain integrand error and domain-migration error. Across 106 successful material–codec–tolerance cases, the identity \(\Delta Q_{\rm total}=\Delta Q_{\rm integrand}+\Delta Q_{\rm domain}\) closes to within \(2.22\times10^{-16}\) e. The bounded domain-dominance fraction \(|\Delta Q_{\rm domain}|/(|\Delta Q_{\rm domain}|+|\Delta Q_{\rm integrand}|)\) has median 0.995 (IQR 0.965–0.999), and 84.9% of cases exceed 0.90. This direct claim is restricted to the representative mechanism set. **b,** Fraction of grid voxels whose Bader label changes after reconstruction as a function of measured \(L_\infty\), illustrating that comparable field-error magnitudes can produce different amounts of partition migration. **c,** Representative spatial example of reference and reconstructed Bader labels, highlighting voxels that change atomic basin. **d,** Full-benchmark mechanism-consistency analysis at the Protocol-A.1 \(10^{-3}\) e contract after conservative complete-case exclusion of all 20 materials with any registered downstream failure at nominal relative tolerance \(\le0.01\). Material-fixed-effect codec multipliers controlling measured \(L_\infty\) are 2.34 for SZ3/ZFP and 2.08 for SPERR/ZFP; after adding the reassigned-voxel fraction they attenuate to 0.96 and 0.94, respectively. The reassignment coefficient is 0.880 (95% CI 0.723–1.037) in log10–log10 units, \(p=5.78\times10^{-28}\). Panel d is interpreted as attenuation/mediation-consistent evidence, not as a formal causal-mediation estimate. Data: `mechanism/basin_error_decomposition_summary.csv`, `analysis_output/complete_case_failure_sensitivity.md` and `paper_data_v03/fig3_complete_case_mechanism_attenuation.csv`. Panel a will be upgraded with the preregistered per-atom analysis when the full table is released.

## Figure 4 | The downstream QoI must be numerically resolvable, and the stability probe itself must be validated

**a,** Fraction of systems classified as NON_EVALUABLE under Protocol A.1 at Bader-charge contracts \(\tau\in\{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2}\}\) e, shown overall and by development/external stratum. Across all 319 systems, 79.9%, 41.4% and 9.7% are non-evaluable at the three contracts, respectively. NON_EVALUABLE is an eligibility status and is not counted as a codec failure. **b,** Stability floor under the archived float32 round-trip probe versus the calibrated five-seed, matched-amplitude uniform-noise probe for the 18-material calibration set. The non-monotone probe produces substantially larger Bader responses because it can disturb local ordering and basin assignments that monotone rounding often preserves. **c,** Algorithmically relevant effects of the two probes in the calibration set. Float32 rounding creates a median 82 exact neighbouring ties, whereas matched-amplitude random noise creates none; zero voxel reassignment occurs in 9/18 materials under float32 and 2/18 under noise. **d,** Predictability audit for Protocol-A.1 eligibility using conventional low-cost metadata. Error bars show development fivefold-cross-validation AUROC variability; the independent external AUROC is 0.409, 0.387 and 0.398 at \(10^{-4},10^{-3}\) and \(10^{-2}\) e, respectively, indicating poor cross-corpus transfer. A sensitivity excluding the single known symmetry-equivalent basin-relabeling case changes the overall non-evaluable fractions by less than 0.3 percentage points at every contract. Data: `stability/`, `analysis_output/resolvability_predictability.csv` and `paper_data_v03/fig4_symmetry_aware_resolvability_sensitivity.csv`.

## Figure 5 | Nominal tolerance separates into bound utilization and a residual associated with reconstruction structure

**a,** Distribution of measured \(L_\infty\) divided by the requested absolute pointwise bound. Median utilization is 0.1575 for ZFP and approximately 1.000 for SZ3 and SPERR, showing that equal nominal tolerance is not equal realized perturbation magnitude. **b,** Conservative complete-case comparison at the central Protocol-A.1 contract \(\tau=10^{-3}\) e. Operating points are matched within material by mutual-nearest measured \(L_\infty\) within 0.10 decades after removing every material with any registered downstream failure at nominal relative tolerance \(\le0.01\). Median re-derived Bader-error ratios remain 1.82 (95% CI 1.68–2.01; 119 materials) for SZ3/ZFP and 2.00 (1.79–2.25; 115 materials) for SPERR/ZFP. Alternative within-material common-support interpolation gives 2.07 and 1.99, respectively. **c,** Material-fixed-effect codec multipliers on the same complete-case scale before and after including Bader voxel reassignment. The approximately twofold codec-associated residual collapses to approximately unity after reassignment is included, linking the matched-error result to the domain-migration mechanism. The adjusted codec coefficient is an association and is not interpreted causally. Data: `benchmark/master_benchmark_full.csv`, `analysis_output/complete_case_failure_sensitivity.csv` and `paper_data_v03/fig3_complete_case_mechanism_attenuation.csv`.

## Figure 6 | Certified compression is a contract-dependent Pareto problem

Median best certified compression ratio plotted against certification coverage among Protocol-A.1-admitted material–codec rows, separately for bulk and slab systems. Each point corresponds to a Bader-charge contract and labels indicate \(\tau\) in electrons. At \(10^{-2}\) e, SZ3 reaches the highest median compression ratio (51.8× bulk; 67.8× slab) but ZFP provides higher coverage (98.8% bulk; 96.7% slab versus 95.2% and 77.0% for SZ3). Near \(10^{-3}\) e, SZ3 and ZFP have similar bulk ratios (12.9× and 13.5×), while ZFP retains higher slab coverage. At \(10^{-4}\) e, ZFP leads the bulk ratio at 7.6× and certifies all 42 admitted bulk rows. Only four slab materials are admitted at \(10^{-4}\) e, so that stratum is descriptive rather than a population-level ranking. NON_EVALUABLE systems are excluded before this frontier is constructed and must not be interpreted as codec failures. Data: `benchmark/best_certified_a1.csv` and `paper_data_v03/fig6_certified_compression_frontier.csv`.
