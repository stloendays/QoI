# Manuscript narrative reframe — 2026-09-09

## Core question

What does a controlled error in an electronic density actually imply for downstream scientific observables?

The manuscript should answer: **not enough by itself**. Scientific fidelity is a property of both the reconstruction and the downstream QoI operator.

## Preferred conceptual statement

`density reconstruction error -> QoI operator -> scientific error`

The same reconstruction is interrogated through three structurally distinct observables:

- total electron count: global linear integral;
- Hartree potential: linear nonlocal operator;
- Bader charge: local integral over density-dependent topological domains.

This is not a ranking of QoIs. It is a controlled comparison of error-propagation structure.

## Three headline findings

### 1. Global conservation does not certify local chemical fidelity

Across 6,343 reconstructions, the electron-count deviation has a median of approximately `9.38e-5 e`, while the resolved Bader-error median is approximately `4.00e-3 e`. More directly, among 3,205 reconstructions with `|Delta N_e| < 1e-4 e`, 1,383 (43.15%) still have resolved Bader error at or above `1e-3 e`.

Safe claim: **even high-precision conservation of total electron number is insufficient to certify atom-resolved charge fidelity.**

Do not claim that total electron number is always preserved by lossy compression; the loose-tolerance tail contains large deviations.

### 2. QoI mathematical structure controls error propagation

The full Hartree expansion uses all 254 development materials, all three codecs and all 6,343 frozen reconstruction rows. After the predeclared reproduction gate, 6,270 rows enter the scientific statistics.

For Hartree-potential error versus realized `L_inf`, the codec × stratum slopes are 0.91–1.12, with a pooled exponent of 1.02. Per-material Hartree `R^2` medians are 0.994–0.997. Resolved Bader error on the identical reconstructions has lower pooled `R^2` in all six codec × stratum cells, materially lower monotonicity, broader local elasticity and extreme single-rung jumps.

At matched Hartree-potential error, 55.4% of gate-passed rows fall in bins for which the Bader-error `P90/P10` is at least 10; such bins occur for every codec and in both bulk and slab strata.

Safe claim: **similar smooth-field fidelity does not uniquely determine topology-dependent local fidelity.**

Slab caveat: strict Hartree rung-by-rung monotonicity is weaker for slabs. The supported slab contrast is based on high per-material `R^2`, smaller local fluctuations, narrower elasticities and much smaller jump amplitudes, not on universal monotonicity.

### 3. A topology-sensitive QoI must be qualified before certification

For Bader charge, re-deriving basins after reconstruction is required. Fixed-basin scoring suppresses the domain-migration term and understates the true downstream error. Bader charge also exhibits a material-specific numerical/topological stability floor; Protocol A.1 therefore determines whether a requested scientific tolerance is evaluable before any compression result is certified.

Safe claim: **a QoI cannot serve as a fidelity contract at a precision at which the QoI itself is not numerically identifiable.**

## Recommended Results order

### Results 1 — Reconstruction error is not a QoI contract

Introduce the three-operator hierarchy and electron-count negative control. Show that global conservation can coexist with local charge failure.

### Results 2 — Smooth and topological QoIs propagate the same perturbation differently

Present the full Hartree expansion. Lead with the near-unit scaling exponent and per-material fits, then show Bader non-monotonicity and matched-Hartree-error dispersion. This is the new general-mechanism section.

### Results 3 — Bader failure is generated by the partition operator

Show why Bader behaves differently: fixed-basin versus re-solved evaluation, domain migration and representative jump cases. This section connects the cross-QoI result to a concrete topological mechanism.

### Results 4 — The Bader contract has an intrinsic stability floor

Present Protocol A.1, the probe correction and evaluability fractions. Make clear that this is a property of the observable/evaluation procedure, not of the codec.

### Results 5 — Honest codec comparison requires realized distortion

Present nominal-versus-realized `L_inf`, matched-realized comparisons and residual codec-dependent error structure.

### Results 6 — Stability-qualified compression remains useful and externally reproducible

Present certified rate–fidelity frontiers, lossless baselines and the final 63-system confirmatory result.

## Abstract architecture

The Abstract should follow this sequence rather than beginning with codec rankings:

1. Scientific datasets are increasingly compressed under pointwise error bounds, but those bounds do not specify preservation of downstream observables.
2. We evaluate 6,343 reconstructions of 254 electronic densities with structurally distinct QoIs and a deeply validated Bader-charge case.
3. Total electron-number conservation does not certify Bader fidelity; Hartree-potential error scales approximately linearly with realized `L_inf`, while Bader error is substantially more non-monotone and can vary by more than a decade at matched Hartree error.
4. For Bader charge, basin migration dominates important failure modes and a material-specific stability floor determines whether a scientific tolerance is evaluable.
5. A stability-qualified certification protocol yields honest codec rate–fidelity tradeoffs and reproduces the pre-specified directional results on all 63 confirmatory systems.
6. Conclusion: lossy scientific data should be certified against the downstream QoI, not only the reconstructed field.

Do not put the 22,296x jump in the Abstract; it is illustrative, not the population-level effect size.

## Discussion architecture

The Discussion should distinguish three layers:

**General evaluation principle.** A field-error norm is not a universal proxy for downstream scientific validity. The operator mapping the field to the scientific quantity matters.

**Mechanistic Bader case.** Bader adds a density-dependent topological domain. The partition can change under perturbations that remain modest under smoother field-level observables, producing non-monotone and discontinuous-looking charge responses.

**Operational consequence.** Scientific compression contracts should specify: `(QoI definition, evaluation procedure, intrinsic stability qualification, acceptable QoI error, codec reconstruction condition)`. Nominal codec tolerance alone is incomplete.

## Claim boundaries

Supported:

- QoI-dependent fidelity on the tested electronic-density observables.
- Smooth Hartree response versus topology-sensitive Bader response on identical reconstructions.
- General evaluation principle: downstream QoI must be part of a scientific compression contract.
- Deep mechanistic and certification claims for Bader charge.

Not supported:

- universal laws for all scientific observables;
- Hartree potential being intrinsically "better" or more scientifically important than Bader;
- universal monotonicity of Hartree error, especially on slabs;
- attribution of every Bader anomaly solely to one geometric feature without the established decomposition evidence;
- a universal codec winner independent of tolerance, realized perturbation and QoI contract.

## Recommended title set

Primary: **Scientific Fidelity Is QoI-Dependent: Certifying Lossy Compression of Electronic Densities**

More chemical/JCTC-forward: **Chemical Fidelity Is QoI-Dependent: Certifying Lossy Compression of Electronic Densities**

More mechanistic: **From Density Error to Chemical Error: QoI-Dependent Failure Modes in Lossy Electronic-Density Compression**

Avoid titles that imply a universal theorem or that make Bader instability the only contribution.

## Manuscript pruning

Move the following out of headline space:

- standalone bulk-versus-slab stability generality figure;
- detailed negative algorithm experiments;
- full probe-seed/amplitude sensitivity;
- full Hartree reproduction/provenance tables;
- exhaustive codec ladder tables.

Keep in main text:

- QoI hierarchy;
- fixed-basin/re-solved mechanism;
- A.1 stability qualification;
- realized-`L_inf` control;
- certified rate–fidelity result;
- external confirmation.

## Decision on Hirshfeld

Do not add Hirshfeld now. Its original purpose was to separate "atomic charge" from "topological partition" sensitivity. The full Hartree expansion already establishes operator-dependent behavior at population scale. Hirshfeld would now be an optional future validation, not a requirement for the present JCTC story. Reopen it only if a reviewer specifically challenges whether the Bader effect is unique to topological partitioning among atom-partition charge schemes.
