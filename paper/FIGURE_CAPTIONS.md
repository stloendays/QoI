# Main-text figure captions

## Figure 1

**Figure 1 | A measurement contract for scientific compression.** The reference density and requested scientific tolerance are evaluated first through QoI Stability Qualification (QSQ). QSQ re-derives the downstream quantity of interest after five pre-specified perturbations and defines a material-specific stability floor. If that floor exceeds the requested tolerance, the material–threshold pair is treated as non-evaluable for robust scientific certification, although fixed-pipeline numerical codec agreement may still be reported. If the reference QoI is eligible, reconstructed densities are evaluated with the same downstream analysis and are classified as certified or eligible but not certified according to whether the reconstruction error satisfies the requested scientific tolerance. The workflow keeps reference stability and reconstruction agreement as separate benchmark axes; QSQ is a finite-panel empirical qualification under the declared perturbation model, not a worst-case stability guarantee.

## Figure 2

**Figure 2 | Downstream operator structure controls how density reconstruction error propagates into scientific observables.** **(A)** Tight electron-count conservation does not guarantee atom-resolved Bader fidelity: 1,383/3,205 reconstructions with absolute electron-count deviation below $10^{-4}\,e$ still have re-derived Bader error of at least $10^{-3}\,e$. **(B)** Hartree-potential error follows an approximately first-order response to realized density perturbation across codecs and material classes, with pooled log–log exponent 1.02. **(C)** Hartree response is substantially smoother than re-derived Bader response at the material level. **(D)** Even among reconstructions with similar Hartree error, Bader errors remain widely dispersed; 55.4% of gate-passing rows lie in 0.5-decade Hartree-error bins with a Bader P90/P10 ratio of at least 10. The comparison motivates explicit treatment of the downstream operator in scientific-compression benchmarks.

## Figure 3

**Figure 3 | QoI Stability Qualification prospectively stratifies downstream numerical risk after equalizing compression-search opportunity.** **(A)** The pre-specified five-seed QSQ screen is challenged prospectively at the primary threshold of $10^{-3}\,e$ using 59 fresh iid-uniform perturbations per development material, with no retuning of the screen, amplitudes or threshold. QSQ admits 143/254 materials (56.3% coverage). Among 8,437 fresh trials from admitted materials, 135 exceed the threshold (1.600%; material-cluster 95% CI 0.782–2.596%), whereas 5,326/6,549 trials from the 111 screen-rejected materials exceed it (81.325%; 75.981–86.257%), giving a rejected-to-eligible risk ratio of 50.83. **(B)** Completing the same four tight settings for the 111 previously uncovered materials adds 1,332/1,332 successful reconstructions; under this equal-search design, no-pass risk at $10^{-3}\,e$ is 3.3% for QSQ-eligible versus 66.4% for screen-rejected material–codec pairs (20.34-fold). **(C)** Fresh-perturbation discrimination remains strong at the pre-specified secondary thresholds: 4.016% versus 86.938% at $10^{-4}\,e$ and 0.148% versus 79.593% at $10^{-2}\,e$. The same 14,986 perturbation trials support all three thresholds. QSQ is interpreted as empirical risk stratification under the declared perturbation model rather than a worst-case stability certificate.

## Figure 4

**Figure 4 | Stability qualification must excite the numerical failure mode of the downstream analysis.** **(A)** QSQ produces substantially larger material-specific stability floors than an order-preserving float32 round-trip control at the same amplitude scale. **(B)** The difference is strongly material dependent rather than a single global rescaling. Across the paired development set, the five-seed QSQ floor is a median of approximately $1.6\timesimes10^4$ times the round-trip response. **(C)** The QSQ floor defines which requested Bader tolerances are numerically measurable across the development and external stability universe. The comparison shows that perturbation magnitude alone is insufficient; the perturbation must probe directions capable of changing the downstream basin assignment.

## Figure 5

**Figure 5 | Topology-dependent basin migration explains irregular amplification of re-derived Bader error.** **(A)** Re-solving Bader basins after reconstruction can amplify the error relative to evaluating the reconstructed density on the reference basin. **(B)** Representative per-atom decompositions separate the total charge change into an integrand contribution on the reference domain and a domain-migration contribution; the latter can dominate large deviations. **(C)** Full tolerance ladders contain abrupt re-derived Bader jumps even as field distortion changes smoothly. **(D)** Basin-reassignment severity is associated with the size of local charge jumps across the development benchmark. The mechanism is specific to density-dependent partitioning; fixed-basin calculations are retained only as diagnostics.

## Figure 6

**Figure 6 | Equal nominal codec tolerance conflates realized distortion magnitude with reconstruction-error structure.** **(A)** At the same requested tolerance, ZFP realizes a median $L_\infty$ perturbation of approximately 0.17 times that of SZ3 or SPERR, whereas SZ3 and SPERR are approximately matched. **(B)** After within-material matching on realized $L_\infty$ using the primary 0.10-dex caliper, re-derived Bader-error ratios remain 0.557 for ZFP/SZ3, 0.601 for ZFP/SPERR and 1.033 for SZ3/SPERR; the ZFP direction is robust across 0.05–0.30 dex sensitivity calipers. **(C)** Compression rate remains a separate axis from downstream fidelity after realized-distortion matching. **(D)** The residual error-structure effect can alter certification rates among jointly QSQ-eligible material pairs. Fair codec comparison therefore requires measured reconstruction distortion rather than nominal codec settings alone.

## Figure 7

**Figure 7 | Frequency allocation of reconstruction error controls Hartree fidelity at matched realized $L_\infty$.** **(A)** Radial reconstruction-error spectra for the exact 457 ZFP/SZ3 within-material matched pairs across 214 materials; curves show median non-zero, Nyquist-safe spectral error-energy fractions and ribbons show pairwise interquartile ranges. ZFP shifts error away from the lowest-$G$ modes relative to SZ3. **(B)** Hartree weighting strongly emphasizes those long-wavelength modes because squared Hartree error scales as $|G|^{-4}$. **(C)** Material-level centers and 95% material-bootstrap intervals for the total spectral-energy factor, spectral Hartree-susceptibility factor and Nyquist-safe Hartree-error ratio are 0.376, 0.203 and 0.07762, respectively; exact multiplicative closure is evaluated pairwise. **(D)** Spectral Hartree susceptibility contributes a material-median 62.0% of the absolute-log codec effect. ZFP has lower spectral Hartree susceptibility in 99.5% of materials, a higher spectral centroid in 98.1%, and a lower low-$G$ error-energy fraction in 99.1%. The reference-implementation Hartree ratio (0.0776221) is reproduced by the Nyquist-safe operator (0.0776219), and the Fourier/real-space identity closes to a maximum relative discrepancy of $1.30\timesimes10^{-15}$. This mechanism is established for the linear Hartree control and is not asserted to be the unique mechanism governing nonlinear Bader error.

## Figure 8

**Figure 8 | Untouched external systems reproduce the stability-qualified decision frontier.** **(A)** Median best-certified compression ratios in the pre-specified 63-system external cohort reproduce the tolerance-dependent codec transition observed in development. **(B)** The ZFP–SZ3 ordering changes with the requested scientific tolerance rather than defining a universal codec ranking. **(C)** Realized distortion remains codec dependent at equal nominal tolerance in the external cohort. **(D)** QSQ eligibility expands from 16/63 systems at $10^{-4}\,e$ to 42/63 at $10^{-3}\,e$ and 57/63 at $10^{-2}\,e$. All 63 systems completed, yielding 1,689 retained scientific rows, zero material-level pipeline failures and zero codec error-bound violations. The external analysis uses the same pre-specified qualification and codec-scoring rules without retuning.
