# Main-text figure captions

## Figure 1

**Figure 1 | Qualification, certification and diagnosis of a scientific measurement contract.** **(A)** The objects the contract acts on, for one development material and one frozen benchmark row (KCN, mp-676693; ZFP at nominal relative tolerance $10^{-3}$, compression ratio 21.6): the reference valence density (isosurface 0.60 $e\,\text{\AA}^{-3}$), its Bader basins (the same isosurface coloured by the owning atom), the decoded error $\tilde\rho-\rho$ (isosurfaces $\pm2.3\times10^{-4}\,e\,\text{\AA}^{-3}$) and the voxels whose Bader owner changes when the basins are re-derived on $\tilde\rho$ (0.42% of voxels). The resulting charge error, $4.4\times10^{-3}\,e$, exceeds $\tau=10^{-3}\,e$ for a material whose stability floor ($2.2\times10^{-5}\,e$) makes it eligible, so this row is eligible and not certified. Renders use one orthographic camera; fields are regenerated from the SHA-256-verified source with the frozen loader and codec, and the re-derived basins reproduce the pinned-stack re-solve of this row exactly. **(B)** The workflow as one track. The measurement contract declares the quantity of interest $Q$, the downstream algorithm, the requested tolerance $\tau$ and which inputs are exact or approximate. Qualification applies the pre-specified probes $\delta_k$ to the approximate inputs, re-derives $Q$ after each and records the stability floor $f_C=\max_k|\Delta Q_k|$; a contract with $f_C\ge\tau$ leaves the main line as non-evaluable. Certification evaluates each decoded field $\tilde\rho$ (ZFP, SZ3, SPERR) with the same analysis: $|\Delta Q|<\tau$ is certified, and an eligible contract with $|\Delta Q|\ge\tau$ for every evaluated setting is not certified. The three lines end in the diagnosis band, which names the mechanism examined for each outcome in this study and the corresponding action: the partition-defining field of Bader analysis for non-evaluable contracts (Fig. 5) and the coupling of the reconstruction-error spectrum to the downstream operator for codec differences (Figs. 6 and 7).

## Figure 2

**Figure 2 | Downstream operator structure controls how density reconstruction error propagates into scientific observables.** **(A)** Tight electron-count conservation does not guarantee atom-resolved Bader fidelity: 1,383/3,205 reconstructions with absolute electron-count deviation below $10^{-4}\,e$ still have re-derived Bader error of at least $10^{-3}\,e$. All 6,343 rows are shown, coloured by local point density, with the count in each quadrant. **(B)** Hartree-potential error follows an approximately first-order response to realized density perturbation: pooled log–log slope 1.02 (95% CI 1.00–1.04; Pearson $r$ = 0.89; 6,270 rows, 254 materials). Right, the slope for each codec and material class; all six lie between 0.91 and 1.12. Confidence intervals are bootstrap intervals that resample materials. **(C)** Hartree response is substantially smoother than re-derived Bader response at the material level. The deviation from a power law, $1-R^2$ of each material's log–log fit, is larger for Bader than for Hartree in 232/241 (ZFP), 202/219 (SZ3) and 212/218 (SPERR) material–codec pairs (Wilcoxon signed-rank $p < 10^{-30}$ for each codec; 678 pairs in total). **(D)** Even among reconstructions with similar Hartree error, Bader errors remain widely dispersed. Each bubble is one 0.5-decade Hartree-error bin (area, number of rows; colour, Bader P90/P10, white at 10); 55.4% of gate-passing rows lie in bins with a Bader P90/P10 ratio of at least 10. The comparison motivates explicit treatment of the downstream operator in scientific-compression benchmarks.

## Figure 3

**Figure 3 | The frozen QSQ screen prospectively stratifies downstream numerical risk.** **(A)** Each of the 254 development materials at its frozen five-probe QSQ stability floor, against the fraction of its 59 fresh iid-uniform perturbations whose re-derived Bader response reaches $\tau=10^{-3}\,e$ (dashed line). QSQ admits the 143 materials with floor $<\tau$ (navy): 135 of their 8,437 fresh trials exceed $\tau$ (1.600%; material-cluster 95% CI 0.782–2.596%). In the 111 screen-rejected materials (red), 5,326 of 6,549 trials exceed it (81.325%; 75.981–86.257%), a rejected-to-eligible risk ratio of 50.83. The screen, amplitudes and threshold were fixed before the fresh trials were drawn. **(B)** Top, the finite-panel admission bound: for a material whose single-probe exceedance probability is $p$, the probability that five exchangeable qualification probes admit it and a later probe exceeds $\tau$ is $p(1-p)^5$, with a ceiling of 6.70% at $p=1/6$. Each material is placed on the curve at its fresh exceedance fraction (navy, eligible; red, screen-rejected). Bottom, materials per 5% bin of that fraction (log scale): 124 materials have no fresh exceedance and 54 exceed $\tau$ in all 59 trials. The observed joint rate over all fresh trials is 0.90% (135/14,986). **(C)** Equal-search control. Completing the same four tight settings for the 111 previously uncovered materials adds 1,332/1,332 reconstructions; points show the no-pass risk of QSQ-eligible (navy) and screen-rejected (red) material–codec pairs (762 decisions per threshold), with the risk ratio: 7.30-fold at $10^{-4}\,e$, 20.34-fold at $10^{-3}\,e$ (3.3% versus 66.4%), and unbounded at $10^{-2}\,e$, where every eligible pair has a numerical pass. **(D)** Fresh exceedance risk at the three pre-specified thresholds with material-cluster 95% bootstrap intervals: 4.016% versus 86.938% at $10^{-4}\,e$ (21.65-fold), 1.600% versus 81.325% at $10^{-3}\,e$ (50.83-fold) and 0.148% versus 79.593% at $10^{-2}\,e$ (537.69-fold). The same 14,986 trials support all three thresholds.

## Figure 4

**Figure 4 | Stability qualification must excite the numerical failure mode of the downstream analysis.** **(A)** Re-derived Bader stability floors of the 254 paired development materials under the order-preserving float32 round-trip control (grey) and under QSQ (navy) at the same amplitude scale, as rainclouds: half-violin, box with median and interquartile range, and every material as a point (log scale). The arrow joins the two medians; the median material-level ratio of QSQ to control floor is approximately $1.6\times10^4$. **(B)** The difference is strongly material dependent rather than a single global rescaling. **(C)** The QSQ floor defines which requested Bader tolerances are numerically measurable across the development and external stability universe. The comparison shows that perturbation magnitude alone is insufficient; the perturbation must probe directions capable of changing the downstream basin assignment.

## Figure 6

**Figure 6 | Equal nominal codec tolerance conflates realized distortion magnitude with reconstruction-error structure.** **(A)** At the same requested tolerance, ZFP realizes a median $L_\infty$ perturbation of approximately 0.17 times that of SZ3 or SPERR, whereas SZ3 and SPERR are approximately matched. **(B)** After within-material matching on realized $L_\infty$ using the primary 0.10-dex caliper, re-derived Bader-error ratios remain 0.557 for ZFP/SZ3, 0.601 for ZFP/SPERR and 1.033 for SZ3/SPERR; the ZFP direction is robust across 0.05–0.30 dex sensitivity calipers. **(C)** Compression rate remains a separate axis from downstream fidelity after realized-distortion matching. **(D)** The residual error-structure effect can alter certification rates among jointly QSQ-eligible material pairs. Fair codec comparison therefore requires measured reconstruction distortion rather than nominal codec settings alone.

## Figure 7

**Figure 7 | Frequency allocation of reconstruction error controls Hartree fidelity at matched realized $L_\infty$.** **(A)** Radial reconstruction-error spectra for the exact 457 ZFP/SZ3 within-material matched pairs across 214 materials; curves show median non-zero, Nyquist-safe spectral error-energy fractions and ribbons show pairwise interquartile ranges. ZFP shifts error away from the lowest-$G$ modes relative to SZ3. **(B)** Hartree weighting strongly emphasizes those long-wavelength modes because squared Hartree error scales as $|G|^{-4}$. **(C)** Material-level centers and 95% material-bootstrap intervals for the total spectral-energy factor, spectral Hartree-susceptibility factor and Nyquist-safe Hartree-error ratio are 0.376, 0.203 and 0.07762, respectively; exact multiplicative closure is evaluated pairwise. **(D)** Spectral Hartree susceptibility contributes a material-median 62.0% of the absolute-log codec effect. ZFP has lower spectral Hartree susceptibility in 99.5% of materials, a higher spectral centroid in 98.1%, and a lower low-$G$ error-energy fraction in 99.1%. The reference-implementation Hartree ratio (0.0776221) is reproduced by the Nyquist-safe operator (0.0776219), and the Fourier/real-space identity closes to a maximum relative discrepancy of $1.30\times10^{-15}$. **(E)** One matched pair in three dimensions (pair 266, KCN mp-676693; ZFP nominal $10^{-3}$ versus SZ3 nominal $10^{-4}$, realized $L_\infty$ 0.04 dex apart), one camera and one isolevel per quantity. At the same decoded-error isolevel ($\pm2.3\times10^{-4}\,e\,\text{\AA}^{-3}$), ZFP error is confined to sparse voxels whereas SZ3 error fills the cell; the Hartree-potential error $\Delta V_H$, computed with the audit's Nyquist-safe Poisson operator, forms long-wavelength SZ3 lobes at $\pm1.56$ meV that ZFP never reaches (maximum 0.49 meV; RMS 0.11 versus 0.73 meV). The pair's ZFP/SZ3 Hartree-error ratio, 0.153, reproduces its frozen value. This mechanism is established for the linear Hartree control and is not asserted to be the unique mechanism governing nonlinear Bader error.

## Figure 8

**Figure 8 | The downstream Hartree operator converts compression diagnosis into design.** **(A)** The periodic Hartree response weights squared reciprocal-space density error as $|G|^{-4}$; under the frozen high-rate scalar-quantization model this gives the first-order allocation $\Delta_G\propto|G|^2$, protecting the long-wavelength modes that dominate Hartree error while allowing progressively coarser high-$G$ quantization. **(B)** Pre-specified matched-storage ablation on 12 engineering materials. The operator-derived exponent $\beta=2$ gives lower Hartree error than operator-blind $\beta=0$ in 12/12 materials; the material-median error ratio is 0.0767. **(C)** Disjoint confirmation on 48 previously unseen materials at relative Hartree RMSE $<10^{-6}$. QOAC-H beats each material's best certified ZFP/SZ3/SPERR baseline in 48/48 cases; the median compression-ratio advantage is 15.016-fold (bootstrap 95% CI, 11.204–21.461), with bulk and slab medians of 11.203-fold and 25.699-fold. All 48 selected QOAC-H reconstructions also satisfy the Nyquist-safe Hartree guardrail. **(D)** Complete 254-material development-population census across Hartree tolerances from $10^{-8}$ to $10^{-3}$. Points are median QOAC-H/best-baseline compression-ratio ratios, thick intervals are the interquartile range and thin intervals are P05–P95; labels give the number of comparable material–tolerance pairs. At $10^{-6}$, QOAC-H wins in 253/253 comparable materials, with median 12.463-fold, P05 4.459-fold and minimum 2.444-fold. QSQ eligibility and availability of a certified frozen baseline define the denominator at each tolerance.
