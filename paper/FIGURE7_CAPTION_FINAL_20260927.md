# Figure 7 caption — Fourier-spectrum mechanism

**Figure 7 | Frequency allocation of reconstruction error controls Hartree fidelity at matched realized $L_\infty$.** **(A)** Radial reconstruction-error spectra for the exact 457 ZFP/SZ3 within-material matched pairs across 214 materials. Curves show the median fraction of non-zero, Nyquist-safe spectral error energy in each normalized reciprocal-space bin, $q=|G|/G_{\max}$; ribbons show the pairwise interquartile range. ZFP shifts error away from the lowest-$G$ modes relative to SZ3. **(B)** The same spectra after Hartree weighting. Because squared Hartree error weights each reciprocal-space mode as $|G|^{-4}$, long-wavelength reconstruction error contributes disproportionately to downstream potential error. **(C)** Material-level centers and 95% material-bootstrap intervals for the total spectral-energy factor, spectral Hartree-susceptibility factor and Nyquist-safe Hartree-error ratio. For each matched pair, the exact decomposition is $R_H=\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{ZFP}}/E_{\mathrm{SZ3}}}\sqrt{S_{H,\mathrm{ZFP}}/S_{H,\mathrm{SZ3}}}$, where $S_H=[\sum_{G\ne0}|\Delta\rho(G)|^2/|G|^4]/[\sum_{G\ne0}|\Delta\rho(G)|^2]$. The separately aggregated material-level centers are 0.376, 0.203 and 0.07762, respectively; because these centers are aggregated separately, exact multiplicative closure is evaluated pairwise rather than by multiplying the displayed centers. **(D)** Distribution of the within-material absolute-log contribution attributed to spectral Hartree susceptibility. The material-median contribution is 62.0%; 99.5% of materials have lower ZFP spectral Hartree susceptibility than SZ3, 98.1% have a higher ZFP spectral centroid, and 99.1% have a lower ZFP low-$G$ error-energy fraction. The historical Hartree ratio (0.0776221) is reproduced by the Nyquist-safe operator (0.0776219), and the Fourier/real-space Parseval check closes to a maximum relative discrepancy of $1.30\times10^{-15}$. The mechanism is established for the linear Hartree control and is not asserted to be the unique mechanism governing the nonlinear, topology-sensitive Bader residual.
