# Supplementary figure captions

## Supplementary Figure S1 | QSQ defines the measurable Bader-fidelity landscape

**A,** Material-level Bader stability responses measured with the order-preserving round-trip control and the five-seed QoI Stability Qualification (QSQ) perturbation procedure. The dashed line marks equality; points above it indicate larger downstream sensitivity under QSQ. **B,** Overall fraction of the 319-system stability universe classified as non-evaluable at the three Bader tolerances under the two probe procedures. **C,** QSQ non-evaluable fractions resolved by development bulk, development slab, external bulk and external vacuum-containing 2D strata. Numerical eligibility depends strongly on the requested tolerance and is measured per material rather than inferred from a universal structural class. A non-evaluable material-threshold pair is neither a codec pass nor a codec failure.

## Supplementary Figure S2 | QSQ probe validation, seed sensitivity and amplitude sensitivity

**A-B,** Eighteen-material calibration comparing the order-preserving round-trip control with the non-order-preserving QSQ perturbation at the same material-specific amplitude. The round-trip control creates exact neighbouring ties and leaves more systems with zero basin reassignment, whereas QSQ more effectively excites the order-dependent Bader partition response. The five-seed log10 floor span in this calibration panel has a median of approximately 0.47 decades and a maximum of approximately 2.4 decades. **C,** Application of the five-seed QSQ procedure across the full 319-system stability corpus. The within-material log10 span has a median of approximately 0.37 decades and a maximum of approximately 3.82 decades; relative to the five-seed maximum, a single primary seed changes the eligibility verdict for 35/319, 17/319 and 2/319 systems at $10^{-4}$, $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-2}\,e$, respectively. **D,** Floor response across a two-decade perturbation-amplitude sweep on the 18 calibration materials. The median change from ×0.1 to ×10 is approximately 0.76 decades. The amplitude sweep is a sensitivity analysis and does not redefine the QSQ procedure.

## Supplementary Figure S3 | Extended operator controls separate global, smooth nonlocal and topology-sensitive QoIs

**A,** Electron-count error versus re-derived Bader-charge error across finite development reconstructions. The dashed thresholds mark $|\Delta N_e|=10^{-4}\,e$ and Bader error $=10^{-3}\,e$; 1,383 of 3,205 reconstructions (43.15%) that satisfy the electron-count threshold still exceed the Bader threshold. **B,** Paired material-level goodness of fit for Hartree-potential and Bader response across 678 material-codec pairs with at least five reproduction-gate-passing rows. **C,** Strict monotonicity resolved by bulk/slab stratum and codec. Hartree response is substantially smoother overall, while the slab result cautions against claiming universal monotonicity. **D,** Dispersion of re-derived Bader error within 0.5-decade Hartree-error bins containing at least 10 rows; 3,452 of 6,229 rows (55.4%) lie in bins with Bader P90/P10 at least 10.

## Supplementary Figure S4 | The strictest certified Bader regime is floor-scale, not a universal plateau

**A,** Re-derived Bader error normalized by the independently measured QSQ stability floor for one highest-rate certified reconstruction per material-codec pair. Points show the median and bars the P10-P90 range. At $10^{-4}\,e$, median error/floor is 1.09 for ZFP, 1.33 for SZ3 and 1.23 for SPERR. **B,** Material-level relation between the QSQ floor and the best-certified re-derived Bader error at the $10^{-4}\,e$ contract ($n=123$ certified material-codec decisions); the dashed diagonal denotes equal scales. **C,** Median and interquartile range of error/floor across the common tight ladder as a function of nominal relative codec tolerance. The strictest certified regime is floor-scale and consistent with an emerging analysis-limited regime, but the data do not establish a universal identity between a compression plateau and the QSQ floor.

## Supplementary Figure S5 | Bader mechanism is robust to an independent implementation

**A,** Exact BaderKit decomposition shows that density-dependent basin migration dominates codec, QSQ and spatial-control perturbations, whereas the order-preserving control is heterogeneous. **B,** Absolute stability floors can shift when basin-assignment semantics change across Bader implementations. **C,** Independent Henkelman on-grid analysis reproduces codec responses nearly one-for-one above the print-resolution region, whereas the near-grid variant broadens the absolute response scale. **D,** Spatial reorganization of codec error produces a modest implementation-robust change in Bader response, while a periodic-shift control is near-null. This deliberately stratified panel is a mechanism/implementation robustness study rather than a prevalence estimate.

## Supplementary Figure S6 | Realized-distortion matching conclusions are robust to the caliper choice

**A,** Re-derived Bader-error ratios across 0.05, 0.10, 0.20 and 0.30 dex within-material matching calipers; points show material-level centers and intervals show 95% material-bootstrap confidence intervals. The vertical marker denotes the primary 0.10-dex analysis. **B,** Wider calipers increase common support for the ZFP comparisons, whereas SZ3/SPERR is already matched throughout. **C,** The primary match keeps realized perturbations close, with the median and P95 larger/smaller realized-$L_\infty$ ratios shown across calipers. ZFP retains lower re-derived Bader error than SZ3 and SPERR after matching, whereas SZ3 and SPERR remain similar.

## Supplementary Figure S7 | Codec-resolved consequences of stability qualification

**A,** Naive failures for ZFP, SZ3 and SPERR decomposed into genuine eligible failures and outcomes that occur on non-evaluable material-threshold pairs. At $10^{-4}\,e$ and $10^{-3}\,e$, more than 93% of naive failures for every codec occur where the reference Bader QoI is not independently resolvable. **B,** Fraction of naive passes that also occur on non-evaluable material-threshold pairs, demonstrating that eligibility qualification invalidates apparent successes as well as apparent failures. **C,** Genuine eligible-failure fraction after conditioning on numerical eligibility; exact counts are reported in Supplementary Table S4. QSQ changes the benchmark label space rather than relaxing the criterion for a particular codec.

## Supplementary Figure S8 | Pre-specified rate-fidelity conclusions reproduce on the untouched external cohort

**A,** Median best-certified compression ratio with 95% material-bootstrap intervals across the external cohort, showing that rate-fidelity ordering changes with the requested scientific contract. **B,** QSQ eligibility expands as the tolerance relaxes within the pre-specified 63-system cohort. **C,** Pairwise SZ3-versus-ZFP win fractions reproduce the tolerance-dependent crossover seen in development. **D,** Certification remains high after conditioning on QSQ eligibility. ## Supplementary Figure S9 | Reference densities of five development materials

Reference valence densities of five of the 254 development materials, spanning an ionic cyanide, an oxynitride, a II–IV–V$_2$ pnictide and an intermetallic bulk crystal from the Materials Project and a clean fcc Co surface slab from NOMAD. Each isosurface encloses the densest 15% of that material's non-vacuum voxels ($\rho>10^{-3}\,e\,\text{\AA}^{-3}$), which places the surfaces at 0.44–0.93 $e\,\text{\AA}^{-3}$. Bulk cells share one oblique camera; the slab is viewed along its in-plane lattice vector $a$. Each cell is framed to fill its slot and is not drawn to a common scale. Under each render: source identifier, grid, number of atoms and the frozen QSQ Bader stability floor $f_m$, which spans three decades across the five materials ($2.2\times10^{-5}$ to $1.9\times10^{-2}\,e$). Densities are regenerated from the SHA-256-verified sources with the frozen development loader.

## Supplementary Figure S10 | A certifying writer reproduces the stability-qualified frontier at a fraction of the exhaustive cost

The shaded band is the pre-declared adoption rule (at least 0.98 of the oracle with at most 2% misses at $10^{-3}\,e$); the adopted bisection writer at $10^{-3}\,e$ (red) retains 98.5% at 16.7 solves. **(B)** Mean end-to-end Bader solves per material over all 254 materials at $10^{-3}\,e$: exhaustive 23.5, scan 17.7, bisection 12.0 and bisection with exact sequential QSQ 10.3; the returned rows, stored sizes and certificates are identical in the last two. **(C)** For each eligible material with a certifiable row, the compression ratio returned by the bisection writer over the oracle ratio. Materials below 1 are drawn individually; those at exactly 1 are counted (45/46, 133/143 and 219/227).
