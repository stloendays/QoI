# Hartree-potential QoI pilot (ZFP, 12 small bulk materials) — 2026-09-09

Independent extension. Reads the frozen `benchmark/master_benchmark_full.csv`
(never modified), Protocol A / A.1 untouched. All outputs in this directory are
generated by `run_hartree_pilot.py`; source-file and reconstruction provenance is
in `provenance.json`.

## 1. Loader fix (why the GitHub pilot failed)

The Materials Project objects at
`materialsproject-parsed.s3.amazonaws.com/chgcars/<task>.json.gz` are a Maggma
S3Store envelope `{fs_id, maggma_store_type: "S3Store", compression: "zlib",
data, task_id}`. After the outer gzip, `data` is already a plain JSON dict: the
pymatgen `Chgcar.as_dict()`. The grid lives at `data.data.total.data` inside a
Monty-encoded numpy array (`{"@module": "numpy", "@class": "array", "dtype":
"float64", "data": <nested list>}`). The workflow's recursive search tested
`np.asarray(x["data"]["total"])` on that dict, got a 0-d object, and never found
the array.

The original benchmark loader was located locally at
`D:/Research/RhoCodec/scripts/fetch_mp_corpus.py::parse_chgcar_json` and is now
extracted verbatim into `analysis/mp_chgcar_loader.py` with sanity checks
(SHA-256, ngrid, npoints, natoms, finiteness, lattice). For all 12 pilot
materials the loaded grid is **bit-identical** to the cached `.npz` grid the
frozen benchmark consumed, the lattice matches, and `value_ptp` matches the
frozen table (`provenance.json`).

## 2. Design

- Materials: the 12 smallest development bulk systems (by `npoints`) with at
  least 5 frozen ZFP rows: mp-1778 BeO, mp-2647013 Mo, mp-214 ZrRu,
  mp-1094905 MgTi3, mp-1007755 HfAu, mp-1219705 PrY, mp-1246548 In(FeN)2,
  mp-10761 FeTe, mp-1226636 CeB2Ir2Rh, mp-560030 CaF2, mp-1246480 CoGeN2,
  mp-11419 ThGa2.
- Codec: ZFP fixed-accuracy (`zfpy` 1.0.1, NumPy 2.3.5, Python 3.12.14, the
  benchmark's own interpreter), tolerance = frozen `nominal_tolerance_absolute`.
- `pilot_rows.csv`: the three required rows per material (tight / middle / loose
  by frozen `realized_Linf`), 36 rows. `full_ladder_rows.csv`: every frozen ZFP
  row of the same materials (127 rows), used for the monotonicity analysis
  because three points cannot establish smoothness.
- Hartree potential: `V(G) = 4π ρ(G)/|G|²`, `G ≠ 0`, `V(G=0) = 0`, on the
  CHGCAR grid with the cell's reciprocal lattice. Metric
  `potential_rel_RMSE = RMS(V_recon − V_orig)/RMS(V_orig)`; also
  `potential_rel_Linf`. Both are gauge- and unit-invariant.
- Bader error, fixed-basin error, reassigned-voxel fraction and compression
  ratio are taken from the frozen row; compression ratio is also recomputed.

The single potential "decrease" (In(FeN)2, 1e-7 → 3e-7) is 1.52e-9 → 1.47e-9,
a 3 % change over a 0.33-decade step; every other step is increasing. Bader
decreases are 1–3 per material and include drops by factors of 2–10 while L∞
grows by a factor of 3, followed by jumps of 100–1800x at a single rung
(PrY, ZrRu, CaF2, ThGa2), i.e. the topology-sensitive behaviour already
documented in the mechanism analysis.

Conditional spread: within a fixed decade of potential error, the Bader error
spans 1.2–2.1 decades (p10–p90) at mid/loose tolerances and 4.9 decades at the
tightest bin, where the A.1 stability floor rather than the perturbation sets
the Bader response.

## 5. Interpretation

- The Hartree potential responds to ZFP perturbation as a smooth, essentially
  linear (slope ≈ 1.0–1.1), monotone functional in every material tested,
  including the two whose Bader charge is unstable at the 1e-3 e contract
  (ThGa2 floor 3.3e-2 e, CeB2Ir2Rh 5.3e-3 e). A pointwise L∞ bound therefore
  *does* control this nonlocal but linear QoI to within a material-dependent
  constant.
- The Bader charge, on the same reconstructions, is non-monotone in 11/12
  materials, with log–log R² as low as 0.17 and single-rung jumps of up to
  three orders of magnitude. Its relation to L∞ is a band, not a curve.
- The two QoIs are correlated (Spearman 0.84) only because both grow with
  tolerance; conditioning on potential error leaves 1–2 decades of Bader
  spread. Knowing the Hartree-potential error does not certify the Bader
  charge, and vice versa.

This supports the hierarchy **smooth nonlocal linear QoI (Hartree potential) →
controlled by L∞; topological QoI (Bader basin charge) → not controlled by L∞**.
It is a pilot on 12 small bulk materials and one codec; it does not measure
prevalence.

## 6. Go / No-Go against the pre-stated criteria

1. Reconstruction reproduction gate: PASS, 127/127 exact.
2. Hartree potential error vs realized L∞ stable and smooth: YES, per-material
   R² ≥ 0.994, slope 1.03–1.09, monotone in 11/12 with the single exception a 3 %
   wiggle.
3. Materially different from the Bader response: YES, Bader monotone in 1/12,
   median R² 0.83, elasticities from −4 to +6, jumps up to 1827x.

**Recommendation: EXPAND** — run the same protocol on the full development bulk
and slab sets with ZFP, SZ3 and SPERR (potential only; Bader stays frozen) to
test whether the L∞-controlled behaviour and the codec-independence of the
potential response hold at population scale. Until that expansion exists, the
manuscript statement should be limited to: "on a 12-material ZFP pilot the
Hartree potential is a smooth, near-linear function of realized L∞ (per-material
R² ≥ 0.99) while the Bader charge on the identical reconstructions is not
(monotone in 1 of 12 materials)". Placement: one paragraph plus one
two-panel supplementary figure; it earns main-text space only if the expansion
reproduces the contrast across codecs and on slabs.
