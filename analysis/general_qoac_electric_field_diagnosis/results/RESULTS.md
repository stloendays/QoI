# General-QOAC electric-field — finite-rate diagnosis results

Status: **COMPLETE, diagnosis only.** The frozen electric-field engineering result and the beta-map result
remain NO-GO; `confirmatory_authorized = false`. Nothing here changes a frozen gate or a frozen number.

Provenance note: the high-rate prediction D1 was derived after the frozen engineering NO-GO was known. It is
an explanation of an observed result, not a prospective confirmation.

Closure: 1,200 recomputed settings (12 materials x beta {0, 1, 1.25, 2} x 25 alpha) reproduce the frozen
Nyquist-safe electric-field error to relative 7.8e-9 and the frozen stream size to within 5 bytes (header
float formatting between the Linux CI and this Windows run).

## D1 — the frozen high-rate theory predicts a small effect

Evaluated exactly on each material's conservative Hermitian-orbit set, the matched-rate RMSE ratio depends
only on the |G| distribution of the modes, not on the density:

| quantity | theory, median (range over 12) | observed (frozen) | frozen gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-field beta=1 / beta=0 | **0.783** (0.696 – 0.790) | 0.806 | < 0.70 |
| E-field beta=1 / beta=2 | **0.913** (0.801 – 0.921) | 0.928 | < 0.90 |
| E-field theoretical optimum | beta = 1 in 12/12 | beta* = 1.25 (beta-map) | — |
| Hartree beta=2 / beta=0 (calibration) | 0.050 | 0.077 | — |

The theory is consistent with the observed effect sizes. Gate A (< 0.70) lies beyond the high-rate
prediction in 11/12 materials. Gate B (< 0.90) lies beyond it in 9/12. The gates were stricter than what
the operator law itself implies for this mode distribution. The same calculation for the Hartree potential
predicts a twenty-fold effect, which is why QOAC-H passed by a wide margin. The difference between the two
operators comes from the mode distribution: |G|^-4 weighting concentrates the error weight on a few low-|G|
modes, while |G|^-2 is close to the 3-D mode density and gives only a ~20% RMSE gain at best.

## D2 — coefficient amplitudes: the regime is far from high-rate

At matched rates, about 90% of the canonical orbits quantize to zero for every beta:

| beta | dead-zone fraction | error share from zeroed coefficients | actual RMSE / high-rate prediction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.893 | 0.154 | 0.750 |
| 1 | 0.903 | 0.352 | 0.388 |
| 2 | 0.910 | 0.476 | 0.188 |

A zeroed coefficient contributes |c|^2, which is far below Delta^2/12 at high |G|. A larger beta pushes
more high-|G| modes into this cheap regime, so the finite-rate optimum moves above the high-rate beta = 1.

## D3 — entropy coding does not mask a beta = 1 advantage

| rate measure | beta1/beta0 | wins | beta1/beta2 | wins | beta1.25/beta1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| actual zlib bytes | 0.772 | 12/12 | 0.980 | 10/12 | 0.991 |
| ideal H0, 32 shells | 0.746 | 12/12 | 1.018 | 1/12 | 0.985 |
| ideal H0, 128 q-bins | 0.736 | 12/12 | 1.025 | 0/12 | 0.982 |

With an ideal entropy coder, beta = 1 gains slightly over beta = 0 and loses its edge over beta = 2. The
32-shell integer-width + zlib packing therefore *helps* beta = 1 slightly; it does not hide an advantage.
Finer shells make no material difference.

## D4 — marginal rate-distortion slopes

At beta = 1, the per-shell Lagrangian slopes -dD_s/dR_s are not equal across |G|: equalizing them implies a
median exponent **1.56** (range 1.22 – 2.07). This agrees with the beta-map finite-rate optimum (1.25) lying
above the high-rate prediction.

## Diagnosis

1. The electric-field operator selects the predicted direction: beta = 1 beats beta = 0 in 12/12, at
   essentially the effect size the high-rate theory predicts. The NO-GO comes from gate thresholds
   stricter than the theory's own prediction, not from a wrong operator law. The frozen NO-GO stands.
2. The electric-field case cannot strongly separate beta = 1 from beta = 2. Even the ideal theory predicts
   <= 9% RMSE difference, and the dead-zone regime moves the finite-rate optimum toward 1.25 – 1.6. A
   cross-operator demonstration needs an operator whose predicted separation is large.
3. Any future General-QOAC test should derive its effect-size gates per material from the D1 expression
   (orbit set only, no outcome data) and freeze them before execution. It should also model the dead zone
   in the rate-allocation law (for example via a finite-rate Laplacian/deadzone model) rather than rely on
   the high-rate approximation.
